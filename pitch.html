For the team to read before pitching. The idea is from our first pitch. The figures come from the budget model and change with it.

Format
Five projects pitch during a free dinner. The funding pool comes from Night-Time Boost, and audience votes decide how it is shared among all five. Guests are invited to ask questions, give feedback and get involved.

The idea

There is a lot of loneliness and disconnectedness in Aarhus. It is hard to go out and make connections unless you already have people. It is harder if you are international, have recently moved here, face a language barrier, and do not know where to start building friendships. With so many choices, it is hard to know how to plan a night out that lets you meet people.

Part of the answer might be improv. Playfulness lowers barriers and lets people have a good time with others in a structured way. The night is facilitated and planned, so people can show up on their own without worrying about what will happen.

  • It is not for improv experts. It is for people who want to play games with strangers and get to know them. Low stakes, and every decision is made for them.
  • Games in a secluded room, similar to the warm-up at the start of the day.
  • Food together, probably street food, with time to talk. Then a few more games.
  • On the way, see a little of Aarhus and its nightlife, which is useful for people new in town.
  • End at a booked bar with a small stage. People can watch improv and go up if they want to. Nobody is pushed on stage.
  • After a few hours the group has inside jokes, and some may feel brave enough to try a short scene together.
  • Afterwards it is an opening into Aarhus Improv, an open community, especially for English speakers. Or simply a fun night out.

What to ask for

Votes. Votes decide our share of the pool, so ask for a share and say what it pays for:

Involvement. The organisers want guests to get involved, so be specific:

  • A free or cheap workshop room.
  • A bar with a small stage that would host the second half of the night for a minimum spend instead of a fee.
  • Volunteer hosts, two or three per night, to welcome people who come alone and walk with the group between venues.
  • Help reaching internationals: language schools, newcomer groups, workplaces with many international staff.
  • People to come to the first night and give feedback.

Two of the other projects from the earlier pitches, the inclusive nature-themed party and the Community Safety Officers, mentioned working together. If they pitch on the day, say that a night out could end at their party, with their awareness team present.

Questions to be ready for

  • How many people per night, how often, and what does a ticket cost?
  • Who facilitates, and are they paid?
  • How do you move a group between venues at night, and what if people drop off?
  • Is it accessible, and is there a sober option for the bar part?
  • How will you know it worked? For example, people coming back, joining Aarhus Improv, or staying in touch.
